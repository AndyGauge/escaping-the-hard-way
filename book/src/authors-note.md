# Author's Note

This book uses direct language about violence, killing, torture, addiction, and death.  I do not soften these words.  I will not call killing "unaliving."  I will not call torture "enhanced interrogation."  I will not call a place where people are sent to die "the farm" except to show you how that language was used to hide what was actually happening.

The normalization of violence begins with the normalization of the words we use to describe it.  When we make death cute enough for an algorithm, we make it small enough to ignore.  I watched an entire country learn to talk about mass killing the way you talk about the weather.  I am watching my own country start to do the same thing.

The sequence of events in this book may not be perfectly accurate.  The trip was ten days and it was a blur.  I did not think to record it at the time because I did not yet understand its impact.  I kept a daily blog while it was happening in real time, but that record has been lost.  What remains is memory, photographs, and the weight of what I carried home.  The events are real.  The people are real.  The order may shift because that is how memory works — it organizes by impact, not by calendar.  The things that hit the hardest are the clearest.  The rest I have reconstructed as honestly as I can.

It was our escape of the hard way that prepared us to face it again.

This book is not safe.  The things in it happened.  The language I use to describe them is the language they deserve.  If that is difficult to read, it should be.  It was difficult to live.
